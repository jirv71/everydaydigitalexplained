Work-From-Home Job Sent You a Check? Is It a Scam, and What Do I Do?

Last verified 2026-10-30 against 5 official sources

Short answer: Yes, it's a scam. The FTC says no honest employer will ever send you a check to deposit and then tell you to send part of the money on, or to buy gift cards with it. The check will bounce, and the bank will want you to repay the full amount. Don't send any money from the check. If you already did, contact the company you used to send it right away and ask to reverse the payment. Then report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

  • Don't send money from a check you got from someone you don't know, even if your bank shows the money as available.

  • A job that has you receive and mail out packages is not a real job. The FTC says reshipping is part of a scam.

Step by step

  1. Know the rule: real jobs don't work this way. The FTC says no honest employer will ever send you a check to deposit and then tell you to send part of the money on, or to buy gift cards with it. If a new job asks you to do that, stop. It's a scam.
  2. Don't pay to get a job. The FTC says honest employers, including the federal government, will never ask you to pay to get a job. Scammers ask you to pay for starter kits, so-called training, or certifications that turn out to be useless.
  3. Say no to reshipping. Some fake jobs ask you to get packages at home, throw away the boxes and receipts, and mail the items on. The FTC says reshipping goods is never a real job. It's just being part of a scam.
  4. Don't trust a check that "cleared". The FTC says banks must make deposited money available quickly by law, but it can take weeks to find out a check is fake. Seeing the money in your account doesn't mean the check is good. Don't send any of it to anyone.
  5. Search the company before you say yes. Search online for the company or the person hiring you, plus words like "scam," "review," or "complaint." The FTC says finding no complaints doesn't prove a company is honest, but finding some can warn you.
  6. Already sent money? Act now. The FTC says to contact the company you used to send the money right away, like your bank, card company, payment app, wire service, or gift card company. Tell them it was a scam and ask them to reverse the payment, if they can.
  7. Report it. Report the job scam to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. The FTC says you can also report it to your state attorney general. For fake checks, the FTC also lists the U.S. Postal Inspection Service.

How the fake check job scam works

The FTC says scammers post job ads the same way honest employers do. You get hired fast, often for a work-from-home job. Then a check arrives, usually for more than you expected.

Next comes the ask. You're told to deposit the check and send part of the money to someone else, or to buy gift cards and send the numbers. The FTC lists fake jobs like mystery shopping and personal assistant work that use this trick.

The FTC says the check will bounce, and the bank will want you to repay the amount of the fake check. By then, the scammer has the money you sent, and you're stuck paying the bank back.

Why a "cleared" check can still be fake

The FTC says that by law, banks have to make deposited money available quickly. So the money can show up in your account in a short time.

But the FTC also says it can take weeks for a bank to figure out that a check is fake. Even if you see the money in your account, that doesn't mean it's a good check.

The FTC's advice: never use money from a check to send gift cards, money orders, or cryptocurrency, or to wire money to anyone who asks you to. Don't rely on money from a check unless you know and trust the person who sent it.

If you already sent money or shared personal details

Paid by credit or debit card: The FTC says to report it to your card company or bank right away and ask for your money back. Paid by wire transfer or a payment app: report it to that company right away and ask them to reverse the payment.

Paid with a gift card: The FTC says to contact the gift card company right away using the number on the back of the card, and to keep the card and receipt. Paid with cryptocurrency: contact the exchange or ATM operator right away. The FTC says crypto payments don't have the same legal protections as cards, so getting money back can be hard.

Gave personal details for "payroll"? The FTC says you may now have an identity theft problem. If you gave out your Social Security number, the FTC says to go to IdentityTheft.gov for steps.

Common questions

Will a real employer ever send me a check and ask for some back?

No. The FTC says no honest potential employer will ever send you a check to deposit and then tell you to send part of the money on, or to buy gift cards with it. If a job asks you to do this, it's a scam.

The money showed up in my account. Doesn't that mean the check is real?

No. The FTC says banks must make deposited money available quickly by law, but it can take weeks to find out a check is fake. Seeing the money doesn't mean the check is good. When it bounces, the bank will want the money back from you.

Do I have to pay the bank back for a fake check?

The FTC says the bank will want you to repay the amount of the fake check. That's why the scam works: the scammer keeps the money you sent, and you're left owing the bank.

Is it normal to pay for training or a starter kit for a new job?

No. The FTC says honest employers, including the federal government, will never ask you to pay to get a job. Scammers ask you to pay for starter kits, so-called training, or certifications that turn out to be useless.

Where do I report a job scam?

Report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. The FTC says you can also report it to your state attorney general. For a fake check, the FTC also lists the U.S. Postal Inspection Service.
