How Do You Read Your Medicare Annual Notice of Change?

Last verified 2026-10-27 against 12 official sources

Short answer: The Annual Notice of Change is the letter your Medicare Advantage Plan or Medicare drug plan must get to you by September 30 each year. It lists changes in coverage, costs, provider networks, service area, and more that start in January. For this year, those changes start January 1, 2027, and they apply to you even if you don't switch plans. Read it with a list of your drugs, doctors, and pharmacies in hand. Then, if anything changed in a way you don't like, compare plans during Open Enrollment, October 15 to December 7, 2026. If you didn't get the notice, contact your plan.

  • The changes in your notice start January 1, 2027, whether or not you switch plans.
  • A Plan Non-renewal Notice is a different letter. If you get one, your Medicare Advantage Plan is leaving Medicare and you need to choose new coverage.
  • Expect a lot of mail and ads around Open Enrollment. Medicare.gov, the "Medicare & You" handbook, and 1-800-MEDICARE are Medicare's official sources of unbiased Medicare information.

Step by step

  1. Find the notice and check the year. Look for mail from your Medicare Advantage Plan or drug plan titled Annual Notice of Change. Make sure it's about 2027. A second item, the Evidence of Coverage, gives the full details of what the plan covers. Your plan sends it, or a notice telling you how to get it online or by mail, by October 15.
  2. Check what you'll pay in 2027. Find the monthly premium, deductibles, copayments, and coinsurance for 2027. In a Medicare Advantage Plan, also find the maximum out-of-pocket limit. That is the most you pay in a year for Part A and Part B services the plan covers.
  3. Look up each of your drugs. Check that every drug you take is still on the plan's drug list for 2027, and see which tier it's in. A drug in a higher tier usually costs more. If a drug was dropped or moved to a higher tier, write it down.
  4. Confirm your doctors, hospitals, and pharmacies. Look for changes to the plan's provider network and service area. Make sure the doctors, hospitals, and pharmacies you use are still in the network for 2027, and that you still live in the plan's service area.
  5. Compare plans before December 7. If something changed that you don't like, go to Medicare.gov/plan-compare to compare your plan with other 2027 plans side by side. During Open Enrollment, October 15 to December 7, 2026, you can switch. The plan must get your request by December 7, and new coverage starts January 1, 2027.
  6. Get free help if anything is unclear. Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) gives free counseling on choosing a plan and understanding costs. Find yours at shiphelp.org. You can also call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227), TTY 1-877-486-2048, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, except some federal holidays.

What the Annual Notice of Change is

Federal rules require Medicare Advantage Plans and Medicare drug plans to send the Annual Notice of Change so it reaches you no later than September 30 each year. Medicare.gov says it includes any changes in coverage, costs, and more that will take effect in January. Medicare's booklet on Medicare Advantage adds provider networks and service area to that list.

The notice is a standard document, so plans use a format set by Medicare. The Evidence of Coverage is a separate, longer document. It explains everything the plan covers and what you pay. Current members must get it, or a notice explaining how to get it online or by mail, by October 15.

If you keep your plan, the changes in the notice still happen. Medicare says that if you keep your current coverage, any changes to benefits or costs for the new year start on January 1. That's why it's worth reading the notice even if you plan to stay.

How to read it without getting lost

Start with your own list: every drug and dose you take, the pharmacies you use, and the doctors and hospitals you want to keep. Then go through the notice one item at a time and mark anything that changes for you. You don't need to understand every page, only the parts that touch your care.

Costs: look at the premium, deductibles, copayments, coinsurance, and, in a Medicare Advantage Plan, the maximum out-of-pocket limit. Drugs: check each drug on the 2027 drug list and its tier. Network and area: check your doctors, hospitals, and pharmacies, and that your home is still in the service area.

Drug lists can also change during the year. Medicare says a drug plan can change its list under Medicare's guidelines, and your plan must tell you about changes that affect drugs you take. If a drug you need isn't on the list, you or your prescriber can ask the plan for an exception.

2027 drug numbers to keep in mind

In 2027, no Medicare drug plan can have a deductible higher than $700, up from $615 in 2026. Some plans have no deductible.

Once your out-of-pocket spending on covered Part D drugs reaches $2,400 in 2027 (it was $2,100 in 2026), you pay nothing out of pocket for covered Part D drugs for the rest of the year.

Medicare-negotiated prices for 15 more Part D drugs take effect January 1, 2027. Medicare says to contact your plan for details on how these prices might affect you.

If your plan is leaving Medicare

Some people get a different letter in October: a Plan Non-renewal Notice. It means your Medicare Advantage Plan is leaving the Medicare program, and you need to find new coverage for next year.

If your plan's contract with Medicare isn't renewed, you can switch to another plan between December 8 and the last day of February of the following year. If you don't join another Medicare Advantage Plan, you return to Original Medicare when your plan ends. You can also make a change during Open Enrollment, which ends December 7, 2026.

Common questions

Do I have to do anything after I read my Annual Notice of Change?

No. If you're happy with your plan after reading it, your coverage continues automatically. Just know that the changes in the notice start January 1, 2027, even if you don't switch.

I never got my Annual Notice of Change. What should I do?

Contact your plan. Medicare.gov says that if you don't get your Annual Notice of Change, you should contact your plan. You can also call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227), TTY 1-877-486-2048, for help.

What's the difference between the Annual Notice of Change and the Evidence of Coverage?

The Annual Notice of Change lists what is changing for next year. The Evidence of Coverage explains everything the plan covers and what you pay. Your plan sends the Evidence of Coverage, or a notice on how to get it online or by mail, by October 15.

My doctor or drug isn't covered next year. Can I switch plans?

Yes, during Open Enrollment, October 15 to December 7, 2026. You can join, switch, or drop a Medicare Advantage Plan or drug plan, or switch to Original Medicare. The plan must get your request by December 7, and your new coverage starts January 1, 2027.

What if I miss December 7?

If you're in a Medicare Advantage Plan, you can change to a different Medicare Advantage Plan, or switch to Original Medicare and join a separate drug plan, from January 1 to March 31, 2027. Otherwise, you generally need to qualify for a Special Enrollment Period, for example if you move out of your plan's service area or your plan leaves Medicare.
