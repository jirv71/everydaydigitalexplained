What Is a Small Succession in Louisiana? The New $200,000 Limit

Last verified 2026-10-30 against 6 official sources

Short answer: In Louisiana, the court process for settling a person's estate is called a succession. A small succession can often be handled with a sworn affidavit instead of a court case. Under Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure article 3421, a person who died domiciled in Louisiana has a small succession if the property they left had a gross value of $200,000 or less at the date of death. Act 293 of the 2026 Regular Session raised that limit from $125,000, effective August 1, 2026. For someone who lived outside Louisiana, the limit for their Louisiana property is still $125,000. Step by step

  1. Check where the person lived. Article 3421 has different rules depending on domicile, which is roughly the state the person treated as home. If they died domiciled in Louisiana, the limit is $200,000. If they lived in another state but owned property in Louisiana, the limit for that Louisiana property is $125,000.
  2. Add up the gross value. List everything the person owned and its value on the date of death. The law uses gross value, so you add up the property itself. Article 3432 asks the affidavit to show the value of each item and the total.
  3. Check for a will. Article 3431 says a small succession doesn't need a court case when a Louisiana resident died without a will. If there is a will, the affidavit route has extra conditions. For example, the will can't leave Louisiana real estate, and the spouse and everyone who inherits under the will must agree.
  4. Find who must sign. Article 3432 says at least two people sign the affidavit under oath, including any surviving spouse and one or more heirs. If there's no spouse and only one heir, a second person who knows the facts must also sign. Heirs who don't sign must be unable to be found, or get 30 days' notice by U.S. mail and not object.
  5. Know what goes in the affidavit. The affidavit lists the date of death, domicile, marital status, the heirs and their addresses, and a description of the property. It also says what share each heir gets. Signers swear under penalty of perjury that it is true, and an heir who signs accepts the succession.
  6. Use and record the affidavit. Under article 3434, banks and others holding the person's money or property can pay or deliver it to the people named in the affidavit. If there is real estate, the affidavit must be recorded in the conveyance records of the parish where the property is.
  7. Talk to a Louisiana attorney. These rules have exceptions and a lot of detail. Before anyone signs a sworn affidavit, it's wise to have a Louisiana attorney look at the facts.

What changed in 2026

Act 293 of the 2026 Regular Session (House Bill 215) amended article 3421, paragraph 1, of the Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure. The legislature's official resume digest says the old law set the small-succession limit at $125,000 for a person domiciled in Louisiana, and the new law raises it to $200,000. The digest says the change took effect August 1, 2026.

Only that one paragraph changed. Article 3421 still sets $125,000 as the limit for Louisiana property left by someone who died domiciled in another state.

The three kinds of small succession

Article 3421 lists three situations. First, the person died domiciled in Louisiana and left property with a gross value of $200,000 or less at the date of death. Second, the person died domiciled outside Louisiana and left Louisiana property worth $125,000 or less at the date of death.

Third, the person died at least 20 years before the small succession affidavit is signed. In that case, the Louisiana property can be of any value.

What the affidavit does

Article 3434 says a multiple original or certified copy of the affidavit is full and sufficient authority for paying money or handing over property to the heirs, legatees and surviving spouse named in it. That covers banks, trust companies, corporations and anyone else holding the property. A payer who acts under the article is released from liability.

A recorded affidavit can be used as evidence about the real estate it describes. Someone who claims to be an heir but was left out of the affidavit has two years from the recording date to sue a third party who bought an interest in that property.

If a small succession does go to court, article 3422 sets court costs at half the usual amount, and a succession representative's pay at no more than five percent of the gross assets.

Common questions

Is probate called something else in Louisiana?

Yes. Louisiana's Code of Civil Procedure calls the process of settling a person's estate a succession. A small succession is one that fits the limits in article 3421.

What is the small succession limit in Louisiana now?

For a person who died domiciled in Louisiana, it is $200,000 or less in gross value at the date of death. Act 293 of 2026 raised it from $125,000, effective August 1, 2026. For a person domiciled elsewhere, the limit for Louisiana property is $125,000.

Does the $200,000 limit apply if the death was before August 1, 2026?

The sources checked don't answer that. The official digest gives only the August 1, 2026 effective date. If the death was before then, ask a Louisiana attorney which limit applies.

Do you need a court case for a small succession?

Often not. Article 3431 says a small succession doesn't have to be opened in court when a Louisiana resident died without a will, and in some cases with a will. The heirs use a sworn small succession affidavit instead.

Can one heir sign the affidavit alone?

No. Article 3432 requires at least two signers. If there's no surviving spouse and only one heir, a second person who has actual knowledge of the facts must also sign.
