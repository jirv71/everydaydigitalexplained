Told to Pay With a Gift Card? Is It a Scam, and What If I Already Did?

Last verified 2026-10-27 against 5 official sources

Short answer: Yes, it's a scam. The FTC says only scammers will tell you to buy a gift card and give them the numbers off the back, and no real business or government agency will ever tell you to pay with one. If you already did, contact the gift card company right away, no matter how long ago it happened, using the number on the back of the card. Tell them a scammer got the card number and PIN, ask for your money back, keep the card and receipt, and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

  • Gift cards are for gifts, not payments. If anyone tells you to buy one to pay a bill, a fine, or a fee, it's a scam.
  • If a caller stays on the phone while you go to the store to buy cards, hang up. The FTC calls this a scam tactic.
  • Don't send more money to anyone who promises to recover your gift card money. The FTC says that's a scam too.

Step by step

  1. Know the rule: gift cards are not payments. The FTC says only scammers will tell you to buy a gift card and give them the numbers off the back. No real business or government agency will ever tell you to pay that way. If anyone asks, stop. It's a scam, no matter what story they tell.
  2. Spot the pressure. Scammers say it's urgent and that something terrible will happen if you don't pay right away. They tell you which card to buy and which store to go to, and they may stay on the phone while you shop. If that happens, hang up.
  3. Never share the number or PIN. The card number and PIN on the back let a scammer take the money on the card, even if you still have the card in your hand. Don't read those numbers to anyone or send a photo of the card.
  4. Already paid? Call the gift card company now. Report it to the company that issued the card right away, no matter how long ago it happened. Use the number on the back of the card or one on the FTC's list. Say a scammer tricked you into giving them the card number and PIN.
  5. Ask for your money back. Ask the company to refund your money. The FTC says some companies might give your money back, so it's worth asking. Apple and Google can freeze the money if it's still on the card.
  6. Keep the card and the receipt. Hold on to the gift card and the store receipt. The FTC says you may need them to follow up on your claim. If you can, take a picture of both with your phone.
  7. Report it to the FTC. Report the scam at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. The FTC says every report makes a difference. If you can't find the card company's contact information, or the company won't talk to you, the FTC says to report that to the FTC too.

Stories scammers use to ask for gift cards

The FTC says gift card scams start with a call, text, email, or social media message. The story changes, but the ask is the same: buy a gift card and read off the numbers.

Common stories the FTC lists: a caller says they're from the IRS, the Social Security Administration, or even the FTC, and says you owe taxes or a fine. Someone says they're tech support from Microsoft or Apple and wants remote access to fix your computer. A "friend or relative" has an emergency and asks you to keep it secret, and with voice cloning they may even sound like your loved one. Others say you won a prize but must pay fees first, or that your utility service will be cut off today.

Two more: a romance scammer you met online asks for gift cards, or someone sends you a check for more than you expected and asks you to send back the difference on a gift card. The FTC says that check will be fake.

The FTC says government agencies never demand payment by gift card, and utility companies don't threaten to cut off service unless you pay immediately. If you're worried about a family member, contact them yourself to check that they're all right.

How to reach gift card companies

The FTC keeps a contact list for gift card companies. These phone numbers come from that page: Amazon 1 (888) 280-4331; American Express 1 (877) 297-4438; Apple and iTunes 1 (800) 275-2273 (say "gift card" to reach a live person); Best Buy 1 (888) 237-8289; ReloadIt 1 (888) 633-9434; Sephora 1 (877) 737-4672; Target 1 (800) 544-2943; Vanilla 1 (833) 322-6760; Visa 1 (800) 847-2911; Walmart 1 (888) 537-5503.

For eBay, Google Play, MoneyPak, and Steam, the FTC says to report online: chat with eBay customer support, report the scam to Google, submit a fraud claim to MoneyPak, or contact Steam Support.

Card not on the list? The FTC says to look for the company's contact information on the card itself or search online for the card issuer. Always double-check a number before you call, and never use a number the scammer gave you.

What reporting does, and what it doesn't

ReportFraud.ftc.gov is the FTC's site for reporting fraud, scams, and bad business practices. The FTC says your report is shared with over 2,000 law enforcers. It can't resolve your individual report, but it uses reports to investigate and bring cases.

If the scam started online, you can also file with the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center at ic3.gov. IC3 calls itself the central hub for reporting cyber-enabled crime and says to file even if you're not sure your complaint qualifies. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.

Watch out for a second scam. The FTC says scammers go after people who already lost money and offer to get it back for a fee. Government agencies and legitimate groups will never ask for money to help you get a refund.

Common questions

Does the IRS or Social Security ever take gift cards?

No. The FTC says scammers pretend to be from the IRS, the Social Security Administration, or even the FTC, but government agencies never demand payment by gift card. If a caller says you owe money and must pay with a gift card, hang up. It's a scam.

Can I get my money back after paying a scammer with a gift card?

Sometimes. The FTC says some gift card companies might give your money back, so it's worth asking. Call the company right away, no matter how long ago it happened. Apple and Google can freeze the money if it's still on the card.

I still have the gift card. Is my money safe?

Not if you shared the numbers. The FTC says the card number and PIN on the back let a scammer get the money, even if you still have the card. Call the gift card company now and keep the card and receipt.

What if my gift card company isn't on the FTC's list?

Look for contact information on the card itself, or search online for the card issuer. If you can't find it, or the company won't talk to you, the FTC says to report that at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Someone says they can get my gift card money back for a fee. Is that real?

No. The FTC says never pay upfront for a refund or help with a refund. Government agencies and legitimate groups will never ask for money to help you get a refund. IC3 says it never contacts people directly for information or money.
